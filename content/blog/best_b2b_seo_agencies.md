Finding a B2B SEO agency that understands long sales cycles, technical buyers, and multi-touch decision-making is a different job from finding a generic SEO provider. Most agencies optimize for quick wins and vanity traffic, and that approach falls apart when your buyer takes months to convert and pulls in five stakeholders along the way.

We reviewed 18 B2B SEO agencies that consistently deliver results for companies selling into complex, considered markets. Each one has a different strength, whether that's demand generation, content-led SEO, or technical audits at scale.

Below is what each agency does best, who they work with, and how to figure out which one fits your business.

{{% tldr %}}
**SEO inside a wider growth program.** NEWMEDIA.COM, Directive Consulting, Powered by Search, Kalungi, and Foundgrove bundle search with web development, paid media, CRO, or go-to-market work.

**B2B and SaaS search specialists.** B2BSEO.io, Skale, SimpleTiger, MADX Digital, Growfusely, and Breaking B2B run technical SEO, content, links, and AI search as one program.

**Content-led.** Grow and Convert, Omniscient Digital, Animalz, Foundation Marketing, and Loudspeaker Marketing start from content strategy and editorial. For large, technically messy sites, look at Onely and iPullRank.

**Pricing varies a lot.** Published monthly rates run from $2,500 (Foundgrove) to more than $50,000 (Kalungi's full-service engagements), and several agencies only quote custom pricing.

**Judge on pipeline, not traffic.** Ask for a case study that ties one page to leads, ask who will actually work on your account, and be wary of guaranteed rankings or long contracts before you've seen a deliverable.
{{% /tldr %}}

## How we picked these B2B SEO agencies

We started with a list of more than 40 agencies that claim B2B SEO experience, then narrowed it down with a few criteria. First, we looked for published case studies that tie SEO work to pipeline or revenue, not just traffic charts. Agencies that only showed ranking screenshots got cut early.

We also weighed how long each agency has worked in B2B specifically, since sales cycles, buyer research habits, and content needs differ a lot from B2C or ecommerce work. Agencies that spread their focus across too many verticals without a clear B2B specialty didn't make the final cut.

Client reviews on Clutch and G2 counted too, along with how open each agency is about pricing and process on its own site. The 18 agencies above made it through all three filters.

## What a strong B2B SEO partner should do

A strong B2B SEO partner starts with your buyer, not your keyword list. That means researching who makes the purchase decision, what they search for at each stage, and which objections come up before a deal closes.

From there, a good partner builds content around your sales cycle instead of generic top-of-funnel topics. They should also handle the technical side (site structure, page speed, and indexing) so the content has a real chance to rank once it's published.

Reporting matters too. A strong partner shows you which pages drive traffic that turns into leads, not just which pages get the most visits. And they should be honest about timelines. B2B SEO takes months to show results, and any partner promising overnight wins is setting you up for disappointment.

## How to choose an agency that understands your sales cycle

Start by asking how the agency researches your buyers before writing a single piece of content. Do they talk to your sales team, review past deals, and look at what your best customers searched for before they became customers? If the answer is vague, they probably treat every client the same way.

Ask for examples of content the agency wrote for a company with a similarly long or complex sales cycle. A blog post that works for a $50-a-month subscription tool reads very differently from one written for a $50,000 annual contract that needs sign-off from procurement, legal, and finance.

It's also worth checking whether the agency understands the difference between top-of-funnel awareness content and bottom-of-funnel content built to move a deal forward. Agencies that only produce one kind usually don't grasp how B2B buying works.

## Questions to ask before you sign an SEO retainer

Before signing anything, ask the agency how they define success. If they only talk about rankings and traffic, keep pushing until they connect the work to pipeline and revenue.

A few other questions worth asking directly:

- Who will work on our account, and will we have access to senior strategists or mostly junior staff?
- What do the first 90 days look like, and what should we expect to see by month three?
- How do you handle content that touches technical or regulated topics?
- What happens if our internal team can't turn around approvals or dev requests quickly?
- Can we see reporting examples from a client in our industry?

A vague answer to any of these is worth a second look before you commit budget.

## What B2B SEO costs, and what changes the price

Most B2B SEO retainers run between $3,000 and $20,000 a month, with enterprise engagements going higher. A few factors push the price up or down.

Scope is the biggest driver. An agency handling technical SEO, content production, and link building charges more than one focused only on content. Industry complexity matters too: fintech, healthcare, and legal content often needs subject-matter review, which adds cost.

Team structure also plays a role. Agencies that put senior strategists on every account tend to charge more than those relying on junior writers with light oversight. And keyword difficulty in your market affects how much work it takes to rank at all, which shows up in the quote.

## When to hire a B2B SEO agency instead of building in-house

Hiring in-house makes sense once you have a steady budget for a full team: a strategist, a writer or two, and someone handling technical SEO. Below that threshold, you're usually paying more for less coverage than an agency gives you.

An agency also makes sense when you need specialized skills fast, like digital PR relationships or a technical audit for a complex site migration. Building that expertise in-house takes months, sometimes years.

In-house teams tend to win when your product is niche enough that outside writers struggle to produce credible content without heavy internal review on every piece. If your team is already correcting most of what an agency sends over, that's a signal to bring the work in-house.

## How to tell whether an agency can drive qualified leads

Traffic and rankings are the easy part to show. Qualified leads are harder, and that's where most agencies get vague. Ask for a case study that shows the full path: keyword targeted, content published, ranking achieved, and the leads or pipeline that came from that specific page.

Agencies that can't produce this usually don't track anything past traffic, which means they don't know whether their work drives revenue. Also ask how they define "qualified". A demo request from a student researching a class project isn't the same as one from a director at a company that fits your ideal customer profile.

If an agency can walk you through its targeting logic, keyword by keyword, and tie it back to your sales team's definition of a good lead, that's a strong sign it understands B2B growth beyond surface metrics.

## Red flags to watch for in an agency pitch

A few warning signs to look for before you sign:

- Promises of guaranteed rankings, or a specific position on Google within a fixed timeframe
- Case studies that show traffic growth but never mention leads, pipeline, or revenue
- Heavy reliance on AI-generated content with little or no editorial review
- Vague answers about who will work on your account
- Pressure to sign a long-term contract before you've seen a single deliverable
- No clear process for researching your buyers and sales cycle before writing content

None of these alone is disqualifying, but two or three together usually mean the agency is optimizing for closing your deal, not for running your campaign.

## How to compare agencies beyond their case studies

Case studies are curated by design, so they only tell part of the story. Ask each agency for a reference client of a similar industry or company size, and actually call them. Ask that reference what the agency is bad at, not just what it's good at.

Look at how each agency talks about failure. Every agency has clients who didn't see results, and the ones worth hiring can explain why, honestly, without blaming the client.

It also helps to compare how each agency thinks, not just what it has done. Ask them to walk through how they'd approach your specific market on the first call. The quality of that thinking often tells you more than any past client logo.
