{{< image "what_yc_is_funding_in_2026/what_yc_is_funding_in_2026_banner.webp" "What Y Combinator Is Funding in 2026: a data breakdown of 208 startups" >}}


Y Combinator's public directory currently lists **208 companies** across the three most recent batches: Summer 2026, Fall 2026, and Winter 2027. We pulled all of them from YC's own search index on **August 11, 2026**, classified every company using YC's own industry taxonomy, and counted what actually got funded rather than what people assume gets funded.

The short version: AI is no longer a category in these batches, it's the substrate. But the more interesting finding is where that AI is pointed. Robotics and physical-world companies now match AI infrastructure company-for-company, and the median YC company in these batches has **two people**.

{{% tldr %}}

**The dataset:** 208 companies from the <a target="_blank" href="https://www.ycombinator.com/companies?batch=Winter%202027&batch=Fall%202026&batch=Summer%202026">official YC directory</a> on August 11, 2026. The split is lopsided: **Summer 2026 has 197**, Fall 2026 has 10, Winter 2027 has 1, because Fall 2026 decisions were not out yet. Read it as a portrait of Summer 2026.

**Top areas:** Other vertical B2B / AI apps **33**, AI infrastructure & data **32**, Robotics & physical AI **32**, Developer tools & agent tooling **24**, Fintech & financial ops **23**, Healthcare & bio **19**.

**Three numbers:** **145 of 208 (69.7%)** carry an AI tag, **148 (71.2%)** are in San Francisco, and the **median team size is 2**.

**Examples:** <a target="_blank" href="https://atomarine.co">Atomarine</a>, <a target="_blank" href="https://tensr.com/">Tensr</a>, <a target="_blank" href="https://www.agentfm.ai/">Agent FM</a>, <a target="_blank" href="https://financialdatasets.ai/">Financial Datasets</a>, <a target="_blank" href="https://tridentsecurity.io/">Trident</a>, <a target="_blank" href="https://www.antropi.world/">Antropi Robotics</a>.

**The deal:** $125,000 for 7% on a post-money SAFE plus $375,000 on an uncapped MFN SAFE (<a target="_blank" href="https://www.ycombinator.com/deal">terms</a>).

## First, a caveat about the three batches

If you open the filtered directory link for these three batches, the results are dominated by one batch, and it's worth understanding why before reading anything into the numbers.

**Summer 2026 accounts for 197 of the 208 companies.** It is the batch YC's own site currently marks as the active one. Fall 2026 contributes just 10 companies and Winter 2027 exactly one.

That's not a signal about batch sizes shrinking or growing. It's a timing artifact. YC's <a target="_blank" href="https://www.ycombinator.com/apply">application page</a> states that the Fall 2026 batch "will take place from October to December in San Francisco," that the on-time deadline "was July 27 at 8pm PT," and that companies applying on time receive decisions by **August 28**. In other words, on the day we pulled this data, most of the Fall 2026 batch had not been decided yet, let alone published. The ten Fall 2026 companies already listed are early admits who chose to make their profiles public.

So treat everything below as a portrait of **Summer 2026**, with a small forward-looking sample from Fall 2026 and Winter 2027. Where a company comes from one of the later batches, we say so.

## Top areas these startups work in

We assigned each of the 208 companies to exactly one primary theme, using YC's own `industry`, `subindustry`, and `tags` fields rather than keyword guessing on marketing copy. Each company appears once, so the counts sum to 208.

{{< image "what_yc_is_funding_in_2026/what_yc_is_funding_in_2026_themes.webp" "Primary theme of all 208 companies across YC Summer 2026, Fall 2026 and Winter 2027" >}}

Two things jump out of that chart.

**Robotics is now as big as AI infrastructure.** Thirty-two companies each. If your mental model of YC is "SaaS with a chatbot bolted on," it is roughly two years out of date. Nearly one in six companies in these batches builds something that moves, senses, or manufactures in the physical world.

**The largest single bucket is a residual.** The 33 companies in "Other vertical B2B / AI apps" are the ones that don't fit a clean technical category because they are AI applied to a specific industry: law firms, restaurants, hotels, auto shops, field service, enterprise IT. This is the "AI-native services company" pattern, and we'll come back to it because it's the most under-discussed trend in the batch.

### How much of this is actually AI?

**145 of 208 companies (69.7%)** carry at least one explicitly AI-related tag in YC's directory. That undercounts reality, because YC's tags are self-reported and inconsistent. Reading the one-line descriptions, the number of companies where an LLM or a learned model is load-bearing is meaningfully higher.

The more useful signal is what kind of AI. **Ten companies** tag themselves with Reinforcement Learning, which is unusually high, and they cluster around a specific idea: building RL environments as a product. {{< link href="https://standardmachines.com" >}}Standard Machines{{< /link >}} is doing "RL Environments For Chip Design," {{< link href="https://www.maingen.ai/" >}}Maingen{{< /link >}} is doing "RL environments for industrial operations," and {{< link href="https://olamlabs.ai" >}}Olam Labs{{< /link >}} is "building multi-agent simulations for model evals and training." Selling the training environment rather than the model is a distinctly 2026 position.

## The broad themes, with the companies in them

### Robotics and physical AI

Thirty-two companies, and the striking thing is how few of them are building a general-purpose humanoid. The batch is mostly picking narrow, unglamorous physical problems and automating them end to end.

**{{< link href="https://tensr.com/" >}}Tensr{{< /link >}}** is the clearest statement of the thesis. Its one-liner is "Fully autonomous robotic factories," and the homepage is blunter: a robotic factory that builds robots, with a 12,000 sqft facility in the Bay Area already online.

{{< image "what_yc_is_funding_in_2026/tensr.webp" "Tensr homepage: a robotic factory that builds robots" >}}

**{{< link href="https://www.antropi.world/" >}}Antropi Robotics{{< /link >}}**, one of the Fall 2026 companies, is the same instinct pointed at machining: "Autonomous CNC factories for faster hardware," pitching 95% machine utilization and continuous 24/7 operation.

{{< image "what_yc_is_funding_in_2026/antropi_robotics.webp" "Antropi Robotics homepage: autonomous CNC factories" >}}

Around those sit companies picking specific environments rather than specific machines. Salem Robotics is "deploying robots for inspections in hazardous spaces, like nuclear." {{< link href="https://www.manifoldindustries.ai/" >}}Manifold{{< /link >}} is building "low-cost, deployment-ready robotic labor for warehouses." {{< link href="https://www.griprobotics.ai" >}}Grip{{< /link >}} is doing "physical AI for waste management." {{< link href="https://www.cosmicrobotics.com/" >}}Cosmic Robotics{{< /link >}} is doing "autonomous construction on Earth and beyond," and {{< link href="https://www.librabots.com/" >}}Libra Robotics{{< /link >}} is narrower still, with autonomous robots for solar farm construction.

There's also a distinct tooling layer forming underneath robotics, which mirrors what happened to web development a decade ago. {{< link href="https://osseus.ai/" >}}Osseus{{< /link >}} calls itself "the intelligent development platform for robotics." {{< link href="https://robocurve.org" >}}Robocurve{{< /link >}} is "evaluating robots in the real world" and {{< link href="https://www.instancelabs.ai/" >}}Instance{{< /link >}} is doing "automated evals for robot policies," which is a real tell: when two companies in one batch sell evaluation infrastructure for a field, that field has enough production deployments to need regression testing. {{< link href="https://enact.company" >}}Enact{{< /link >}} is "the post-training layer for robotics," and {{< link href="https://www.neuromorphic.vision/" >}}Neuromorphic{{< /link >}} is "building embodiment agnostic robot brains."

### AI infrastructure and data

Also 32 companies, but the composition has shifted away from "we host models for you." Very few of these are inference-serving plays. Instead there are three recognizable sub-patterns.

The first is **compute as a traded commodity**. {{< link href="https://getcomputable.com" >}}Computable{{< /link >}} lets you "buy, sell, and redeem GPU hours for any week with instant liquidity," and {{< link href="https://touchmark.ai" >}}Touchmark{{< /link >}} is building a "market for future inference capacity." Both are effectively forward markets for compute, which only makes sense if you believe compute pricing is volatile enough to hedge. If you want the current state of the hardware those contracts settle against, we covered it in {{< link href="/blog/fastest_ai_inference_hardware/" >}}Fastest AI Inference Hardware{{< /link >}}.

The second is **cost and portability pressure**. {{< link href="https://www.conifer.build" >}}Conifer{{< /link >}} is a "local-first least cost routing system to reduce 80%+ token spend," {{< link href="https://tracerml.ai" >}}Tracer{{< /link >}} is "combining open-source AI models for better answers at lower cost," and {{< link href="https://understudylabs.com/" >}}Understudy Labs{{< /link >}} helps you "effortlessly move to open weight models." That last one is a business built entirely on the assumption that teams want off the frontier APIs. We wrote about the routing layer these depend on in {{< link href="/blog/best_ai_llm_routers_openrouter_alternatives/" >}}Best AI LLM Routers{{< /link >}}, and about the models they'd switch to in {{< link href="/blog/best_open_source_self_hosted_llms_for_coding/" >}}Best Open Source Self-Hosted LLMs for Coding{{< /link >}}.

The third is **agent-specific infrastructure**, which barely existed as a category two batches ago. {{< link href="https://onecli.sh" >}}OneCLI{{< /link >}} is "the identity gateway for AI agents." {{< link href="https://www.useclick.ai" >}}Click{{< /link >}} and {{< link href="https://context.dev" >}}Context.dev{{< /link >}} both sell web access and realtime context to agents. {{< link href="https://rindler.ai" >}}Rindler{{< /link >}} describes itself as "the translation layer between AI agents and the web." Machine0 sells "cloud computers for AI agents."

At the deep end, {{< link href="https://baudlabs.ai" >}}Baud{{< /link >}} is building "AI chips for ultra-fast model training and inference," and {{< link href="https://parasma.com" >}}Parasma{{< /link >}} is "training human brain cells for AI compute," which is the most speculative company in the entire dataset.

### Developer tools and agent tooling

Twenty-four companies, and the center of gravity has moved decisively from "help humans write code" to "help humans supervise machines that write code."

**{{< link href="https://www.agentfm.ai/" >}}Agent FM{{< /link >}}** is the cleanest example. Its pitch is "one group chat to hear and steer your coding agents," and the product is a command center for live audio updates, computer use, and group chat with agents running across your repos. The screenshot shows agents named Vera, Kai, and Otto reporting in from different branches and models.

{{< image "what_yc_is_funding_in_2026/agent_fm.webp" "Agent FM homepage: an interface between what you want and your coding agents" >}}

The same assumption, that you are now running many agents rather than one, shows up repeatedly. {{< link href="https://jcode.sh/" >}}Jcode{{< /link >}} promises "20x more coding agents, 20x more productivity." {{< link href="http://hyperprobe.co" >}}HyperProbe{{< /link >}}'s line is sharper: "Your coding agent writes code. Now let it fix prod too." {{< link href="https://www.archal.ai/" >}}Archal{{< /link >}} is "the improvement loop for AI agents," {{< link href="https://agnost.ai" >}}Agnost AI{{< /link >}} is "product analytics for AI agents," and {{< link href="https://heybuildbox.com" >}}Buildbox{{< /link >}} helps you "understand how users experience your AI agents." {{< link href="https://codag.ai/" >}}Codag{{< /link >}} is doing "log compression for agents," and {{< link href="https://amulet.so/" >}}Amulet{{< /link >}} is building a "high performance file system for agents," both of which are the kind of unglamorous plumbing you only build once a workload is real.

{{< link href="http://coasty.ai/" >}}Coasty{{< /link >}} is worth calling out separately because it names the hardest problem in the space: "the computer-use agent that doesn't break on real software." If you're comparing the underlying agents these tools wrap, see {{< link href="/blog/best_open_source_cli_coding_agents/" >}}Best Open Source CLI Coding Agents{{< /link >}} and {{< link href="/blog/best_ai_tools_for_coding/" >}}Best AI Tools for Coding{{< /link >}}. Several of these products also expose themselves to agents over MCP, which we walk through in {{< link href="/blog/expose_mcp_server_with_pinggy/" >}}How to Expose an MCP Server{{< /link >}}.

{{< image "what_yc_is_funding_in_2026/coasty.webp" "Coasty homepage: computer use that sees any screen" >}}

### Fintech and financial operations

Twenty-three companies, and this is where the "AI-native services firm" pattern is most obvious. Rather than selling software to accountants, several of these companies are becoming the accounting firm. {{< link href="https://thebillow.ai/" >}}Billow AI Labs{{< /link >}} bills itself as an "AI-native Accounting Firm to Replace the Big-4," and {{< link href="https://lastaccountingcompany.com/" >}}Last Accounting Company{{< /link >}} is an "agent-native accounting firm." {{< link href="https://florin.inc" >}}Florin{{< /link >}} is "the insurance carrier with zero underwriters," which is the same move applied to insurance.

The other half is infrastructure for agents that handle money. **{{< link href="https://financialdatasets.ai/" >}}Financial Datasets{{< /link >}}** sells exactly what its name suggests, market infrastructure built so agents can query the stock market. Its homepage even carries a HUMAN / AGENT toggle at the bottom, which is a small but telling design decision.

{{< image "what_yc_is_funding_in_2026/financial_datasets.webp" "Financial Datasets homepage: connect your agent to the stock market" >}}

{{< link href="https://agentcard.sh" >}}Agentcard{{< /link >}} is doing "debit cards for AI agents," and {{< link href="https://zommalabs.com" >}}Zomma{{< /link >}} is building "computer use agents for back offices in finance." {{< link href="https://prodigy.markets" >}}Prodigy Research{{< /link >}} is training "the world's best foundation model for quantitative finance."

### Healthcare and bio

Nineteen companies, split between operations and discovery. On the operations side the pattern matches fintech: {{< link href="https://radley.health/" >}}Radley{{< /link >}} is an "AI-native, full-stack radiology practice," {{< link href="https://trydenta.com" >}}Denta{{< /link >}} is "the dental practice that runs itself," and {{< link href="https://cova.care/" >}}Cova{{< /link >}} is an "AI-native home care agency." These are service businesses, not software vendors.

{{< image "what_yc_is_funding_in_2026/radley.webp" "Radley homepage: an AI-native radiology practice" >}}

On the science side, {{< link href="https://tarebio.com/" >}}TareBio{{< /link >}} is working on "vaccines for any cancer or infection in weeks," {{< link href="https://atlasdiscovery.bio" >}}Atlas Discovery{{< /link >}} is "predicting human response to drugs in clinical trials," and {{< link href="https://www.rasyn.ai/" >}}Rasyn{{< /link >}} is working "towards a general intelligence for chemistry."

### Security and compliance

Only eight companies, which is smaller than you might expect given how much attack surface the rest of the batch is creating. What's notable is that most of them are securing AI systems rather than using AI to secure conventional ones.

**{{< link href="https://tridentsecurity.io/" >}}Trident{{< /link >}}** sells continuous AI pentesting for apps, APIs, and cloud, with the neat framing that "findings return as patches" rather than as a PDF.

{{< image "what_yc_is_funding_in_2026/trident.webp" "Trident homepage: 24/7 security, not once a year" >}}

{{< link href="https://fabraix.com" >}}Fabraix{{< /link >}} calls itself "the world's frontier hacker for AI agents." {{< link href="https://trytrust.ai" >}}TrustAI{{< /link >}} does "continuous compliance and governance for agents on sensitive systems." {{< link href="https://www.traceforce.ai" >}}Traceforce{{< /link >}} is "securing AI native apps directly on devices," and {{< link href="https://www.datoric.com" >}}Datoric{{< /link >}} is selling "security-first training data." For a concrete example of why this category exists, see our writeup on {{< link href="/blog/agentjacking_ai_coding_agents_sentry_mcp/" >}}agentjacking and AI coding agents{{< /link >}}.

{{< image "what_yc_is_funding_in_2026/fabraix.webp" "Fabraix homepage: agents that hack your AI before attackers do" >}}

### Industrial, energy, and defense

These are small buckets individually, 10 and 6 companies, but together they represent the batch's clearest bet that AI demand becomes an energy and manufacturing problem.

**{{< link href="https://atomarine.co" >}}Atomarine{{< /link >}}** is the most literal version: offshore nuclear-powered data centers, floating platforms with standalone power and seawater cooling.

{{< image "what_yc_is_funding_in_2026/atomarine.webp" "Atomarine homepage: nuclear data centers at sea" >}}

Nearby, {{< link href="https://gopacific.ai/" >}}Pacific{{< /link >}} is "mass-producing and deploying micro data centers for Earth and Space," {{< link href="https://www.propriorobotics.com/" >}}Proprio Robotics{{< /link >}} is "building autonomous data centers," and {{< link href="https://marengox.com" >}}Marengo{{< /link >}} is "automating engineering design, starting with data centers." Four separate companies attacking data center buildout from four angles is not a coincidence.

{{< image "what_yc_is_funding_in_2026/pacific.webp" "Pacific homepage: edge compute deployed in days, anywhere in the world" >}}

The defense companies are unusually direct about what they are. Isengard Industries builds "mass-produced AI strike systems and Counter-UAS." {{< link href="https://greypointindustries.com/" >}}Greypoint Industries{{< /link >}} is "manufacturing the future of autonomous swarm warfare." {{< link href="https://earendil.io" >}}Earendil Robotics{{< /link >}} does "drone swarm defence at a small-unit level," and {{< link href="https://hop-aero.com" >}}Hop Aero{{< /link >}} is doing "rocket cargo delivery to contested environments." A decade ago YC funded almost nothing in this category.

The live edition is for **Fall 2026** and contains 13 categories. Comparing it against what actually got funded in Summer 2026 is a useful reality check, and the hit rate is higher than you'd expect.

## Where these companies are, and how small they are

Two structural facts are worth stating plainly, because they shape what "getting into YC" now looks like.

**It is overwhelmingly a San Francisco program.** 148 of 208 companies (71.2%) list San Francisco as their location, and 183 (88.0%) are in the United States. New York is a distant second at 12 companies, followed by Boston with 7, and London and Los Angeles with 5 each. YC's application page states the Fall 2026 batch "will take place from October to December in San Francisco."

**Teams are tiny.** The median team size is **2**, the mean is 2.7, and 146 of the 208 companies (70.2%) have three people or fewer. The largest team in the dataset has 30 people, and it is an outlier. A two-person team shipping an AI-native product to a vertical industry is the modal YC company in 2026.

## How to use this if you're building

If you're evaluating your own idea against these batches, the process is fairly mechanical.

**Start by checking whether your category is saturated or empty.** Filter the YC directory for the tags closest to your idea. Twenty-four developer-tools companies means a crowded, well-understood market where differentiation has to be sharp. Five consumer companies against an explicit RFS request means the opposite.

**Then read the RFS against the actual batch, not on its own.** The seven well-covered categories above tell you what YC has already validated with a check. The six thin ones tell you where they have appetite that founders haven't met yet. Those are different bets with different risks: the first is competitive, the second may be thin because the problem is genuinely hard.

**Check the deal terms before you model dilution.** YC's <a target="_blank" href="https://www.ycombinator.com/deal">standard deal</a> is $500,000 total, split as $125,000 for a fixed 7% on a post-money SAFE plus $375,000 on an uncapped MFN SAFE that converts on the terms of the lowest-cap SAFE issued before your priced round. YC charges no fees.

**Finally, note the timing.** The Fall 2026 on-time deadline was July 27, 2026 at 8pm PT, with decisions by August 28. YC says it still accepts late applications, without a guaranteed response timeline.

One practical note for anyone building an agent-facing product in these categories: a large fraction of these companies need to expose a local service, webhook endpoint, or MCP server to something running outside their laptop. ## Conclusion

These batches aren't an "AI wave" any more, because there's nothing left for AI to be a wave against. Nearly 70% of the companies tag themselves as AI and most of the rest use it anyway, so the useful distinctions are downstream of that: **robotics has caught up with software infrastructure** at 32 companies each, **the AI-native services firm** is a real pattern with startups becoming the accounting firm and the radiology practice rather than selling them tools, and **agent supervision is now its own tooling market**.

Worth watching: consumer AI and crypto both have partner-written RFS entries and almost nothing funded behind them. And the caveat bears repeating - 197 of these 208 companies are Summer 2026, so once the Fall decisions land in late August this picture is worth redrawing.
